# Obviously & AI (2023, ~10 months)

**Client:** Obviously (influencer-marketing SaaS; acquired by WPP/VML)
**Role:** Director of Design (was Lead Designer)
**One-liner:** Asked "Can AI fix this?", honestly concluded "not yet" (summer 2023), and delivered a pragmatic three-phase efficiency program using AI precursors — algorithms, automation, and a light-touch AI "first pass."

## The situation
About a decade in, Obviously was acquired by WPP (via VML). The client base
shifted from a diverse set of smaller companies with light, linear campaign needs
to **mega multinational corporations** demanding non-linear, dynamic workflows.
That burden scaled disproportionately on both human labor and software
flexibility, making **efficiency** the urgent mandate.

## "Can AI fix this?" — the discovery
Leadership's natural question was whether AI could replace the manual work. Phong
and the PM ran a discovery project:

1. **Audited existing processes** — mapped every team member to the task flow and
   found the real time sinks.
2. **Interviewed all job roles** — day-to-day, meetings, worries/anxieties, goals.
3. **Took AI vendor calls** — vetted any and all AI vendors.
4. **Audited competitors** — what they marketed, promised, and could actually
   deliver.

**Honest synthesis: "Not really — AI isn't there yet."** Instead, Phong scoped a
three-phase program to raise efficiency using AI *precursors* (algorithms and
machine learning) plus workflow changes.

## The three phases
**Phase 1 — Breaking the Anxiety Loop.** The biggest discovery: the software
tasks themselves weren't slow; the hidden cost was **keeping the team and clients
informed**. As clients grew larger and more anxious, every team member down the
chain chased big and small issues to reassure them — and status/time tracking was
itself a time sink. Since everything ultimately happens in the software, Phong
used **heuristics on in-app actions** to gauge campaign health against timelines,
auto-tracked time from who-clicked-what-when, and programmed the team's intuited
thresholds into warnings/notifications (e.g. "clients worry if fewer than 80% are
hired within 24 hours of the deadline"). Homepages and every campaign/contract
detail page were refactored to surface progress and thresholds — **eliminating
dozens of spreadsheet trackers and the daily meetings and Slack updates.**

**Phase 2 — Automate the Repetition.** Interviews surfaced the "hacks" the team
used, like repeated creator searches that kept surfacing the same people. The
software had assumed one search was enough (clients used to keep static creator
lists), but clients now wanted a **fresh pool each time**. Phong automated dozens
of these repetitive tasks — "not AI, but incredibly effective." The ML backlog
went further: convert the team's intuition and special search parameters into
tags, crunch Obviously's exclusive data (performance metrics, history) into
creator profiles, and apply them across the whole network to **enrich every
search** — plus use Phase-1 data to optimize contract construction and tune the
warning thresholds dynamically over time.

**Phase 3 — A Smarter "First Pass" with AI & Contractors.** Only here does AI
proper come in. The key insight about LLMs: they have little bearing on
Obviously's specialized workflows. The business collects novel creator content
(so no asset generation needed), strategists own prompting with clients, and
client comms are campaign-specific over 3–6 months and hard to automate — any
trained AI would also have to keep each client/contract/campaign uncontaminated.
So AI was applied **lightly, as a "first pass"**: never making official changes
or client communications, only drafting suggestions/findings for a human to
verify — e.g. parsing captions into spell-checker-style suggestions, or digesting
a creative brief into starter ideas for hired creators. Modest by design, "that's
the state of AI when we looked in the summer of 2023" — with RAG and federated
client data as the path to more ambitious automation.

## Takeaway (his conclusion)
The lesson wasn't about the cutting edge: by **distilling older principles and
technologies — algorithms, automations, workflow modifications — you achieve what
the cutting edge promises.** Less sexy, but it adds up to the efficiencies a
business actually needs.

design, automation, human-in-the-loop AI design, change management around AI
adoption.
